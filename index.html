The Studio
Founded by Janet Velez.

Moments Curated is built on a simple belief — that celebrations should feel personal before they feel impressive.

Nothing here is anonymous. Every celebration is shaped by Janet’s own hand — her eye for what matters, and her belief that no one should have to carry their most meaningful day alone.

“I ask about the person long before I ask about the party.”
Janet Velez · Founder & Creative Director
Character is easier to show than to claim.

A few true things about how Janet works — not what she has done, but how she pays attention.

The first question she asks
“Tell me about them. Who are they, and what makes them feel most like themselves?”

Not the age, the color, or the theme. Before any of that, she wants the person underneath — what they light up over, what they do for others, what they would never ask for themselves.

What she listens for
“People tell me what they want. I listen for what they mean.”

For her own daughter Vanessa’s first birthday — a little girl born early, whose first year felt especially precious — the theme was “ONEder the Sea.” But what Janet heard underneath was tenderness. So the ocean stopped being a theme and became an atmosphere: softer palette, rounder shapes, gentler light. No one asked for that. It was what the celebration meant.

What she will not compromise
“What can we remove, so the person becomes even more visible?”

Budgets shift and plans change; she can always redesign. What she protects is the feeling that the celebration was made for the one at its center. Given the choice between more decoration and more meaning, she chooses meaning — keeping every detail that draws the eye to the person, and letting go of anything that only fills the room.

What is always with her
A notebook — not for sketches, but for what she notices.

A sentence someone said. A color that held a memory. A detail she doesn’t want to lose. It is full of unfinished things, because attention is fragile — and sometimes a line written months ago becomes the reason a celebration feels personal. She collects before she composes.

Now think of the person you came here for.

The hope is simple — that someone else might notice what matters about them, the way you already do.
